## Quick Facts

The Nagon are a very communal, hare like species. They come from the Archipelago of Joshu and follow the twelve ascensions. While on a macro scale the Nagon are very independent, meaning that there is very little centralisation in the archipelago, they are intensely communal towards their chosen group. They place immense value on personal experiences and urge every young adult to undergo a sort of pilgrimage in order to explore the world and experience as much as there is to experience. 


## Home Region

They come from the [archipelago of Joshu](../../Continents/Joshu.md), a temperate if close to subtropical array of volcanic islands. They are famous for their warm and humid summers, often times catching unaware travellers of guard and their bitingly cold winters. The volcanic nature of the islands results in a thriving ecosystem, rich in various lavish plants and hugely diverse sea life. There are few mammals on the islands themselves, with those few exceptions living only on the larger islands far away from the coasts. 


## Culture

The communal approach to life every Nagon grows up on, also reflects itself in their political structure, which is structured highly independent on a macro level but tight-knit on the interpersonal plane. Every community has their own council of wise people, with each council made up of an appropriately sized amount of seats.
The amount of seats is dependent on the decision the community made for themselves and usually fluctuates with the amount of people they consider eligible. Each Nagon is raised with the belief that each object or creature is precious and important, be it an ant or one of the wisest Nagon who has ever lived. All are equally important and contribute to the world in their own way. This belief is what they
call ”Júẑòn mánò nígó” or ”Peace of the Twelve” Every member of a community is pushed towards fulfilling the twelve ascensions roughly categorized in four regions:

- Independence
    - Being able to be self-sufficient
    - Work on yourself for the betterment of all
	- Honour and respect yourself and others
- Lifestyle
    - Physical fitness and development of skills
	- Social gatherings to foster communal spirit, teach and learn
	- Fashion as an expression of one’s own experiences and knowledge, combining different styles and colours to tell their life’s story
- Mindset
	- Adventure and experiences for personal development
	- Mindfulness and reflection to prepare and deal with past and future situations
	- Mentorship for others and thus growth for one self
- Interpersonal relationships
	- Inclusivity through respect and openness
	- Conflict resolution through patience and adherence to the core tenants of the community
	- Understand someone else first before passing judgement


## Language

The Nagon language is a tonally focused language containing 5 distinct vowels with 4 individual variations and 16 consonants. Nagon is a syllabic language utilizing symbols denoting a combination of any consonant with any vowel. Since names are culturally very important to the Nagon an astute observer can pick out any Nagon's birth name just by extracting the words with uneven amounts of syllables. This practise is possible due to the Nagon birth names being required to contain either 3, 5 or 7 syllables which no other class of word is allowed to have. More information can be found in [the official Nagon language entry.](../../Languages/Joshu/Nagon.md)

## Religion

There is no ratified or centrally structured religious system, which could be attributed to the whole species of the Nagon. Rather general strokes and similarities can be drawn between the different islands of the Joshu Archipelago. 


### Core Structure

Almost all of the religious practises found in the archipelago follow the belief that everything found in the world has a spirit or soul inside of it. Meaning that the fish one has caught is part of the spiritual web of the world just as much as the tree which was felled to build a home. While the way in which a sacrifice may be honoured differs from community to community, it can be said, that every Nagon, regardless of acclaim, age and community knows the ways their people honour a sacrifice by heart. Each community has set rituals for anything and everything. There are rituals for preparing a berry bush for harvest, as well as a ritual for welcoming a guest into another persons house. In each situation there are multiple spririts which need to be spoken to and just about every community has set rules for all possible encounters. These rules though, are far from set in stone, they are more of a guide line, something to orient an individual while allowing for specific alterations depending on circumstance. It is also permissible to forgo the honouring temporarily if the individual is currently in danger but won't be seperated from the ritualee. 


### Communal Differences

Since there are countless rituals on countless isles it would be an elven life time's worth of time to describe all of them in detail. Still, the general differences can be roughly sketched out. For example, if a Nagon from the Island of Šōnādánádémùnèlá catches a Bowl-fish, they will be required to honour the bowl-fish's spirit either immediately or if serious danger is possible, they will also be able to perform the ritual when having moored their fishing vessel, before anything is permitted to leave the vessel. This specific ritual would require the Nagon take a pinch of sea salt, taken from the sea at most ten days prior and rub the stomach of the bowl-fish in a elyptical pattern. The rubbing would have to be done for the duration of eight stanzas. The stanzas in question would be the poem to thank the stars for their light specifically dedicated to the period the Nagon fisher would currently be in. They ahve to be performed in a specific cadence meaning that each rub round will take about the same amount of time. 


## Physiology

### Body Structure

The typical Nagon body will reach about 150 centimetres in height, 60 kilogramms in weight and about 5 centimetres of für length. For outsiders there is almost no perceivable difference between a male and female Nagon. They possess very strong joints, ligaments and bones, especially in the lower half of their bodies. Their legs are short but powerful, mainly oriented towards performing medium to long jumps as a means of transportation. Although their legs are able to produce a slow wabble, mimicking the upright gait of other species, it is something usually avoided by all Nagon. 


### Internal Organs

The Nagon species is well known for its stocky and squat appearance, but below such surface observations the Nagon internal organism can be described as specialized. Due to their restrictive diet, their stomachs are, compared to other species with respect to differing body proportions, far below average in size, resulting in smaller more frequent meals. Due to the scarcity of gluten based crops and an absence of cattle, the Nagon organs did not adapt to either process lactose or gluten. If a Nagon by accident or willingly consumes gluten or lactose they can expect, depending on dosage, all the usual reactions from a light intolerance up to a full allergic reaction with potential for death. The ancient fish, mushroom and fruit based diet of the Nagon has resulted in extraordinary kindeys, livers and stomach acids. These allow the Nagon to not only quickly digest but also imbibe a host of different poisons in amounts lethal to many other species. 


## Cuisine

### Classic Nagon Cuisine

#### Máŝúnè

One of the few islands that are not only large enough to support extensive permanent farming but also have the soil quality for farmable crops. This has caused early tribespeople to develop a stronger focus on utilising wheats and vegetables in their diet. Many of the baked goods that circulate around the archipelago have their origins stem from the artisan bakers of Másúnè and have since been further developed by other cooks and bakers with creations often times still possessing traceable similarities to their Másúnian origins. 

#### Šōnādánádémùnèlá

The island which is widely known for its excellent fish is often regarded as a pioneer in all things fish. From the use of fish in food or for ornamentation and jewlery. This saw Šōnādánádémùnèlá develop a large variety of almost entirely fish based foods. Due to the rather poor soil, fruits and vegetables were basically non existant in the earlier centuries leading this island's tribe to rely exclusively on fish and the rare plant dredged up from the seas. 

#### Dúẑòlàné

The capital city of the Archipelago has always been a relatively cosmopolitan place. Nagon from all over the Archipelago make their home there either temporarily or permanently at some point in their lives. This means that even before the wider world had opened to them, the local cuisine was already a bright mixture of everything the archipelago had to offer. Still even in the capital most food then was sea based, with a heavy emphasis of different fruits on the side.
